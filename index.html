Ocean Ears

Sound Swimming with Whales

An immersive dive into the acoustic world of whales — making the ocean's hidden soundscape audible, tangible, and worth protecting.

Listening beyond the limits of human hearing.

OCEAN EARS places whale song at the centre of an underwater orchestra — the geophony of earthquakes, storms and waves, the biophony of marine life, and the anthropophony of human-made noise. Rendered in hyperreal, spatial detail, these sounds let people sense the reality whales actually live in.

Drawing on more than two decades of continuous hydrophone recordings and the work of marine bioacoustician communities and research groups, the project transforms specialist scientific data into a sonic archive — one you can listen to, not just look at.

At its core, OCEAN EARS is a collective project. It brings researchers, scientists, artists and the public together around a shared way of listening, and turns that listening into the strategies and capacities needed to act. Rising levels of human-made noise now threaten the acoustic conditions marine life depends on. To protect that world we first have to be able to hear it, and then to answer it as a community.

The Archive

An intelligent, geo-tagged library of marine sound — organised by species, place, time and call type, carefully attributed, and built for science, conservation and listening.

The Application

A browser-based listening map. Swim through 3D soundscapes, turn your head toward a singing whale, move closer, and hear the ocean change across seasons and years.

The Installations

Physical spaces where whale song is felt as well as heard — infrasound below the threshold of hearing, delivered through the body as vibration.

The Listening Map.

Dive in and listen.

An interactive XR map of the ocean's sound is being built now. You'll swim with whales across the Indian Ocean, the Southern Ocean and beyond — listening in space, across seasons and across years.
